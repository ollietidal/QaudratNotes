1. Getting started

QuadratNotes runs in your phone's web browser and works without signal once it has been opened once. Your data is stored only on your phone until you export or back it up.

  1. Open quadratnotes.com while you have signal.
  2. Add it to your home screen so it opens like an app:
    • iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Do this: Safari can clear data from websites you haven't opened for a while, but not from apps on the Home Screen.
    • Android (Chrome): tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.
  3. When asked, allow location, so each quadrat gets its GPS position.

Before your first real survey, try a test survey at home: start one, save a quadrat, export it and check you can open the files.

2. Starting a survey

A survey is one site visit. On the home screen, tap Start a survey under Seagrass, Rocky shore, Saltmarsh or Custom survey, then fill in:

Site, Date, Surveyor
The site name is used in file names and survey codes, e.g. porth-llyn-2026-11-21.
Region
Sets which species are listed. For seagrass it's the seagrass bioregion; for rocky shore and saltmarsh choose NE Atlantic, or Elsewhere for broad groups. Custom surveys use a template instead (see below).
Quadrat size
25 × 25 cm, 50 × 50 cm, 1 × 1 m, or Other for any square quadrat. Densities are worked out from this.
Sampling design
How quadrats were placed: Random, Haphazard, Non-random, Along transects, Fixed plots or Other. Tap the i for definitions. Random and Fixed plots offer guidance to points (sections 6 and 7).
Conditions
Optional and needs signal: Get current conditions looks up weather and a modelled tide for your location.

Tap Start survey. To carry on later, use Continue on the home screen, or the Surveys button at the top.

3. Recording a quadrat

The Record tab shows one quadrat at a time. The quadrat number goes up automatically.

Position
Filled in from live GPS, with its accuracy shown. You can type coordinates instead (decimal degrees); tap Use live GPS to go back.
Cover with the grid
The grid has 25 squares, each 4% of the quadrat. Tap the squares the species or habitat occupies, or type an Exact %. Grid squares are saved and exported, so they also give a frequency out of 25.
Species
Tap the species present. Show more species lists rarer ones, and Add a species not listed lets you type others (separate several with commas).
Photos and notes
Take photo or Choose from phone. Photos are resized to save space. Notes are free text.

Tap Save quadrat. It's stored on the phone straight away.

4. Habitat by habitat

Seagrass

Total seagrass cover (grid or %), species present, shoot count, canopy height (add each measurement), epiphytes, algae cover and sediment. With several species in a quadrat, each gets its own cover grid; a square can hold more than one species. Use None (0%) for a quadrat without seagrass.

Rocky shore

Shore zone, optional height above chart datum, substrate and whether the quadrat is in a rock pool. Seaweeds and attached animals are recorded as % cover in a canopy layer and an understorey layer (each up to 100%, with bare rock counting towards the understorey); mobile animals like limpets and topshells as counts. Species stay listed from quadrat to quadrat, and a species left at 0 is recorded as absent.

Saltmarsh

Marsh zone, optional elevation, % cover of each plant (plants overlap, so totals can exceed 100%), with the Domin class shown. Below 4% cover, pick few, several or many plants. Also bare ground, litter and strandline, vegetation height and grazing or trampling.

Custom survey

For any other habitat, on land or water: a meadow, dune slack, heath or school field. You make a template with your own species list and choose one way to score them:

% cover
Type a % or use the grid. Plants overlap, so totals can exceed 100%.
Count
Individuals in the quadrat; a density per m² is worked out on export.
Present / absent
Tap the species you find. Every other species on the list is recorded as absent in that quadrat.
Cover scale
Pick a Domin (1–10) or Braun-Blanquet (r, +, 1–5) class for each species.

Also count (optional) is a second list scored as counts whatever the main scoring, e.g. snails, woodlice or ants in a % cover survey. In the field, Add something to count adds more, and Add a species not listed adds to the main list. Both stay listed for the rest of the survey.

You can also add quadrat details, recorded once per quadrat: a number with a unit (soil depth, cm), a choice list with one answer per quadrat (aspect: N, E, S, W), yes/no (grazed) or free text. Transects, random points and fixed points work as in the other surveys.

  • On the setup screen, tap New template, fill it in and Save template. Templates stay on the phone for next time; Edit changes them.
  • Share as a file saves the template as a small file. Send it to your team; they tap Import a template file, so everyone records the same way.
  • Each survey keeps a copy of its template, so editing a template later doesn't change surveys already recorded.
  • Use scientific names where you can: they're what databases match on. Check scientific names (needs signal) looks each name up in the GBIF species list and flags spelling slips and names it doesn't know, with suggestions you can tap to use. Common names aren't checked.
  • The data export zip includes each survey's template in a templates folder, so whoever gets the data can see how it was recorded and reuse the template.

5. Checking and editing

The Data tab lists the quadrats in a survey with a summary. Tap a quadrat to open and edit it, then save again, or delete it. Use the Survey menu at the top to switch surveys, and Delete this survey to remove one.

6. Random points

For random sampling, the app can place the points and guide you to each one.

  1. Choose Random as the sampling design and tick Pick random points on a map.
  2. Mark the survey area: Tap on the map at each corner (needs signal for the map), or Walk the boundary and tap Add corner here at each corner (works without signal).
  3. Choose the Number of points and an optional Minimum gap, tap Pick random points, then Use these points.
  4. On the Record tab, the arrow, distance and compass guide you to each point. When it says you've arrived, place the quadrat and save it; it's linked to the point. Use Skip point (with a reason) if a point is underwater, unsafe or can't be reached.

Tip: draw the area before you lose signal. Points, the arrow and recording all work offline.

7. Fixed points

For monitoring or restoration plots you return to over time. Choose Fixed plots and tick Guide me to fixed points. Add points in any of these ways:

Mark this spot
Stand on the plot and tap Mark this spot. The app averages GPS readings for 20 seconds (keep the phone still), or tap Save now to stop early. Type a name first, or use the suggested P01, P02…
Tap the map
Tick Add a point wherever I tap the map, then tap. Useful for planning; less accurate than marking on site.
Import a file
A CSV with point_id, latitude and longitude in decimal degrees (e.g. 52.87123, -4.61234), or GeoJSON or KML. Shapefiles and British National Grid coordinates need converting first: in QGIS, save the layer as GeoJSON or KML in WGS 84.
Copy from earlier
Reuse the points from an earlier survey of the site. Names stay the same, so your data lines up from visit to visit.

Then tap Go next to a point (or the point on the map) to be guided there. Download these points (.csv) saves a file you can import next time or share with a colleague.

Phone GPS gets you to the right area (usually within 3–10 m), not the exact spot. For plots you need to find to the centimetre, use a physical marker or an RTK receiver.

8. GPS tips

  • GPS takes a minute or so to settle after you start, and is worse under cliffs, near buildings and in the first few readings. The accuracy is shown next to the position.
  • An external Bluetooth GNSS, DGPS or RTK receiver set up as your phone's location source is used automatically, and its accuracy is recorded to the centimetre.
  • Height from GPS is exported exactly as the phone reports it. It's usually only good to ±10–30 m and isn't relative to chart datum, so convert and check it before use.

9. Backing up

Your surveys live only on your phone. If the phone is lost, or the browser's data is cleared, they're gone, so back up after every survey.

  • On the Export tab, tap Back up everything (.zip) and save the file somewhere safe, such as your email, Google Drive or iCloud.
  • Restore from a backup adds the surveys in a backup to the phone without changing anything already there. You can also use it to combine surveys from several phones. Custom survey templates are included.

10. Exporting your data

On the Export tab, choose This survey or All surveys, then:

Download data and photos (.zip)
The quadrats table and species table (CSV), photos, and any random or fixed points tables and map file. Join the two tables in R on quadrat_id.
More formats
Map files: GeoJSON for QGIS, ArcGIS and R, or KML for Google Earth. For OBIS / GBIF: a Darwin Core archive, with options for the licence, credit, location precision and surveyor names; nothing is uploaded. Photo records: how to share the exported photos on iNaturalist.

On a phone, the file goes to your Downloads or the share sheet: send it to email or cloud storage to open it on a computer. Every column is described in the export guide.

11. Problems and questions

Location is blocked or not working
The app shows steps for your phone. Usually: on iPhone, Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services, and allow it for Safari (or the app). On Android, check location is on and allowed for Chrome in its site settings. Then reopen the app.
The map is blank
Map images need signal. Without it, points, outlines, the arrow and recording still work; set up areas and points before you lose signal.
An import says the file has grid coordinates
Convert to latitude and longitude in decimal degrees first, for example in QGIS (save as GeoJSON or KML in WGS 84).
A photo has no location in the export
Photos take their location from the quadrat. If the quadrat had no GPS position, the photo has none either.
"New version ready"
An update has been downloaded. Tap Reload once you've saved the quadrat you're on.
My surveys have disappeared
On iPhone, Safari can clear data for websites that haven't been opened for a while; add the app to the Home Screen to prevent this. Restore your latest backup from the Export tab.
Something else, or an idea
Tap the i next to "field testers wanted" on the home screen to send feedback. See also the privacy notice.
