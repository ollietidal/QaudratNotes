About QuadratNotes

QuadratNotes is a free app for recording coastal quadrat surveys of seagrass, rocky shores and saltmarsh, built by a marine biologist for coastal surveys.

Everything you record stays on this device. There are no accounts, no tracking and no analytics, and nothing leaves your phone unless you export it yourself.

If you send feedback from the field-testers note, only your rating, what you type and the app version are sent.

If you tap Get current conditions, your approximate location (to about 1 km) is sent to Open-Meteo to look up the weather and tide. Weather and tide data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0).

The app is free and always will be. If it saves you time, you can support its development.

Tip
Draw the area before you lose signal, at home or in the car park. On site without signal the map background may be blank, but the outline, points and arrow all still work.
GPS accuracy
Phone GPS is usually accurate to 3–10 m (the screen shows the current figure), so treat each point as where to drop the quadrat, not an exact spot. It's worse under cliffs and in the first minute or so. You count as at a point when you're within the current accuracy (2–10 m), and a quadrat saved within 25 m is linked to it with the actual distance recorded.
External GPS receivers
A Bluetooth GNSS, DGPS or RTK receiver set up as the phone or tablet's location source is used automatically, and its accuracy is recorded down to the centimetre.
Adding points
Stand on each plot and mark it (the app averages GPS for 20 seconds), tap the map, import a file, or copy the points from an earlier survey of the same site.
Files
CSV with point_id, latitude and longitude in decimal degrees (e.g. 52.87123, -4.61234), GeoJSON or KML. To use a shapefile or British National Grid coordinates, save them as GeoJSON or KML in WGS 84 first, for example in QGIS.
Accuracy
Phone GPS is usually accurate to 3–10 m, so a saved point gets you to the right area rather than the exact spot. For plots you need to find to the centimetre, use a physical marker or an RTK receiver.

        Species present

        Grid figures are the share of squares a species occurs in, not cover judged by eye: a square with a few shoots counts the same as a dense mat. Where species share a square, each gets that square, so together they can add up to more than the total, which counts each square once.

        Shore zone
        Splash zone
        Above most high tides, wetted by spray. Black and orange lichens, small periwinkles.
        Upper shore
        Covered only around high water. Channelled and spiral wrack, Chthamalus barnacles.
        Mid shore
        Covered and uncovered every tide. Bladder or egg wrack, barnacles, limpets, topshells.
        Lower shore
        Uncovered only around low water. Serrated wrack, red seaweed turfs.
        Sublittoral fringe
        Exposed only on low spring tides. Kelps and their holdfasts.
        Substrate
        Species
        Canopy
        Fronds of larger seaweeds, such as wracks and kelps, lying over the rock. Estimate their cover looking down on the quadrat.
        Understorey
        Everything attached to the rock beneath: barnacles, mussels, turfs, crusts and bare rock. Lift or part the canopy to estimate it.
        Totals
        Each layer can be up to 100%, so canopy and understorey together can add up to more than 100%.
        Counts
        Mobile animals such as limpets and topshells are counted. Use the + and − buttons, or type a number.
        Carrying over
        Species you pick stay listed for the next quadrat. Leave them at 0 if they're not there; that records an absence.

        Marsh zone
        Pioneer
        Open mud at the seaward edge with scattered glasswort, cordgrass or seablite.
        Low marsh
        Flooded by most tides. Saltmarsh-grass and sea purslane, often along creeks.
        Mid marsh
        Flooded by spring tides. Sea lavender, thrift, sea plantain, sea arrowgrass.
        Upper marsh
        Flooded only by the highest tides. Red fescue, sea rush, sea couch.
        Transition
        Grading into land: brackish reedbed, rushes or grassland.

        Signs, animals and surface cover
        Signs
        Most mudflat animals are buried, so count what they leave at the surface: casts, tubes, siphon holes, burrows and feeding marks. Exports record these as signs, not individuals.
        Sub-samples
        For dense, tiny things such as mud snails, count inside a smaller square and choose its size. The app scales the count up to the quadrat and to per m².
        Surface cover
        Algal mats, diatom film and patches of mussels or seagrass, as % of the quadrat. These can overlap.
        Carrying over
        Anything you pick stays listed for the next quadrat. Leave it at 0 if it's not there; that records an absence.

          More formats

          Map files

          One point per quadrat with its details and species, plus the survey area and random points if you used them. GeoJSON opens in QGIS, ArcGIS and R; KML opens in Google Earth and Google My Maps.

          For OBIS / GBIF

          For sharing with biodiversity databases such as OBIS and GBIF. This downloads a Darwin Core file to your phone; nothing is uploaded. Send it on yourself, for example to EurOBIS, and check it first with GBIF's data validator if you like.

          Location precision

          Photo records

          iNaturalist is a free community where people share photos of wildlife and help each other identify them. Records the community agrees on are shared with GBIF. Photos in the data and photos zip are already set up for it: each carries its quadrat's location, time and species list, so the iNaturalist uploader fills in the place and date. Add one organism per observation, and tick Captive / cultivated for planted seagrass.

          Back up and restore

          A backup holds every survey, quadrat and photo on this phone in one file. Save it somewhere safe, such as your email, Google Drive or iCloud.

          Restoring adds the surveys from a backup to this phone without changing anything already here, so you can also use it to combine surveys from several phones.

          On iPhone and iPad, add QuadratNotes to your Home Screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen). Safari can clear data from websites you haven't opened for a while, but not from apps on the Home Screen.
